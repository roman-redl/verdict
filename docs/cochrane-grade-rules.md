# Cochrane and GRADE mechanical rules, and their status in verdict

This is a checklist of the mechanical rules that the Cochrane Handbook and GRADE apply on the way from a set of studies to a pooled estimate and a certainty rating, with the status of each rule in verdict. It is meant for deciding what to build next in the pooling and rating code and for explaining why a given answer was wrong.

Sources: Cochrane Handbook v6.5, chapters 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 13, 14, 15, 23 and 24; the Core GRADE series (parts 1 to 6); GRADE guidance 34, 35 and 36; the GRADE Book. Every quote was checked against the chapter text. Verdict status was read from the code (`internal/pipeline`, `internal/meta`, `internal/grade`) and from [how-it-works.md](how-it-works.md).

## Legend

Kind, in the "kind" column:

- CODE: deterministic given the extracted numbers and the study metadata.
- MODEL: needs reading the paper; the model answers a narrow question and code aggregates the answers.
- N/A: not meaningful for an automated tool.
- JUDGEMENT: the Handbook or GRADE authors themselves leave the decision to a human (flagged inside the kind cell).

Status, in the "status in verdict" column:

- done: implemented as the rule says.
- partial: implemented in part, or only inside a model prompt (what is missing is named).
- missing: not implemented.
- contradicted: the code does the opposite of the rule (even if by omission).
- n/a: the rule does not apply to what verdict outputs.

Known failures are question ids from the benchmark runs (MedEvidence questions, plus the internal sets such as `set40c`). "(recorded)" means the evaluation analysis attributes the failure to this cause; "(inferred)" means it is a reading of the code, not a recorded attribution; "latent" means no failure was recorded, but the code is exposed to the problem.

## What verdict does today, in short

- The critic reads each paper and extracts one effect per paper (counts, means, or a reported estimate with CI, plus free-text outcome and timepoint) and a `study_design`. Go pools the effects (`pool.go`). A study enters the pool if its relevance is `direct`, it is not retracted and its design is not a review. The design is not used otherwise: cluster, crossover, cohort and unknown designs all pool together.
- One report per trial is kept only through shared trial registration numbers (`trials.go`, `pool_trials.go`).
- The pool is chosen among candidate groups by measure (RR, OR from counts, HR, SMD, MD per unit); the group with the most participants wins and the others go to `left_out`. The model is DerSimonian-Laird with a normal 95% CI for k >= 2; one study is shown alone.
- Imprecision levels and effect direction are computed in Go (`internal/grade`). The final verdict level (strong, moderate, weak, insufficient), risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, publication bias, rating up and the starting level are decided by the synthesis model from a prompt (`prompts.go`). For inconsistency, Go only supplies the count of studies with usable numbers on the outcome (`inconsistency_basis`); below 2 the prompt forbids a rating down.

## Rules that interact and must be implemented together

- The unit of a pool is the triple (trial, outcome, time point) inside one design stratum: B1, B5, B6, A1, A2 and B10. The report kept for a trial depends on which report carries the target outcome and time; dropping repeats first (current order in `candidateGroups`) and choosing the time point afterwards loses the right report (245, A1).
- Design stratification (B1, B2) changes everything after it: the starting level (E1), the estimate type (B3 adjusted estimates for non-randomized studies, generic inverse variance), rating up (E15, only for the non-randomized stratum), risk-of-bias gates (B4), and which stratum is the headline. If the randomized stratum has no usable numbers, the fallback to the non-randomized stratum must start at low, not high.
- Cluster adjustment (A7) feeds the weights, I^2 (E5), the participants of the information-size rules (E9, E10, E12) and the dominating-study check (D9). Implementing E9 or E5 before A7 repeats the 163, 260, 273 errors.
- CI method (D3, D4), imprecision (E8, E9) and inconsistency (E5, E6) are one chain: a normal interval at k = 2 or 3 makes imprecision too lenient, and the GRADE Book asks not to charge the same spread twice (E6).
- Risk of bias by weight (E3) needs the pool weights (D2) and a re-pool of the low-risk studies (D9); E4 (objective outcome) changes which studies count as high risk.
- Measure and polarity (B10, B11, B12, B15): scale direction must be fixed before SMD, event polarity before RR, and the OR to SMD conversion only after the same-construct question is answered.
- Level arithmetic (E2) needs every domain to return 0, 1 or 2 with one reason; it must come last and must keep the rule "one problem counts in one domain only" (GRADE Book).
- Provenance (C13) has to run before pooling: a computed or placeholder number that enters the pool corrupts every later rule.

## Data the critic would have to extract in addition

Today, per paper, the critic returns: study design, relevance, one effect (counts, means, or estimate with CI and level, reversed flag, unit, free-text outcome and timepoint), randomized N, strengths and concerns, design quality. The rules above additionally need:

- Outcome identity: normalized outcome construct, whether it matches the question's outcome (primary, secondary, surrogate, composite), event polarity (favourable or adverse), metric (final, change, adjusted, log).
- Time: numeric time point with unit and follow-up length.
- Scale: name, range, direction of benefit.
- Design detail: random versus quasi-random allocation; for cluster trials the number of clusters, mean cluster size, ICC if printed, and whether the reported analysis accounts for clustering; for crossover trials a paired estimate or SD of differences and a first-period-only flag; all arms with their numbers for multi-arm and factorial trials.
- Non-randomized studies: adjusted estimate with CI, adjustment set, crude estimate.
- Reports: report type and "same participants as paper X" (trial name, registration number, follow-up of, erratum or comment on).
- Risk of bias per study and outcome as narrow answers: sequence generation, concealment, blinding of outcome assessors, missing outcome data share per arm, selective reporting, objective outcome yes or no, analysis population (ITT or not).
- Sponsor type and registration or results status (publication bias).
- Statistics other than counts and means: SE or CI of an arm mean, exact P value, median with quartiles, control-arm risk, total events.
- Indirectness as per-element flags: population, intervention (dose, class), comparator type, outcome surrogate.
- The minimal important difference when the paper states one.
- A check field: for every number, the sentence it came from (supports the provenance check C13).

## Where the sources disagree

- Starting level of non-randomized evidence: Core GRADE starts low; Cochrane ch14 and ch24 start high with ROBINS-I and rate down by two for confounding and selection bias. The authors say the end result should align.
- Combining designs: Cochrane ch24 forbids pooling randomized and non-randomized studies; GRADE rates a body of evidence per outcome and has no pooling rule. Cochrane also says pooling cluster and parallel (and crossover and parallel) trials is allowed but best in separate subgroups.
- I^2: Cochrane ch10 gives bands (0 to 40, 30 to 60, 50 to 90, 75 to 100) and the Q test at P below 0.10 but also says "use of simple thresholds ... should be avoided"; Core GRADE gives only "seldom serious below 30 percent"; guidance 36 calls the popular bands inappropriate; the GRADE Book calls I^2 "very preliminary". Two levels for inconsistency: Core GRADE says it need not concern users; the GRADE Book allows it when estimates are separated by more than one threshold.
- Imprecision numbers: the CI ratio cutoffs are RR 3 and OR 2.5 in Core GRADE 2 and guidance 34, reversed in a 2025 empirical check (Murad); the large-effect trigger for the information-size check is 30, 40 or 50 percent depending on the source; the continuous rule of thumb is 800 total (Core GRADE 2, guidance 34) versus 400 (GRADE handbook 2013); guidance 34 and the Book allow three levels, Core GRADE 2 up to two. Relative thresholds RR 0.75 and 1.25 are a Cochrane "very rough guide"; the new GRADE guidance prefers absolute thresholds with baseline risk.
- Risk of bias: Core GRADE gives weight thresholds (65 percent, 55 percent, 35 to 45 percent) but no rule for how many high-risk items make a study high risk ("no definitive way") nor for acceptable missing data ("no valid rule of thumb"); Cochrane's mapping of "some concerns" is "no limitation or serious", that is, left to the user; Core GRADE has only low or high per study, no "unclear".
- Publication bias: Core GRADE and Cochrane treat small industry-funded studies as a signal; the GRADE Book says not to assume it by default. Maximum one level (2013 handbook) versus two or three under exploration (Book).
- Fixed versus random effects: the Handbook gives no universal recommendation; the tau^2 estimator and CI method advice (REML, Hartung-Knapp) is newer than the DerSimonian-Laird plus normal interval that most tools still use.
- Number of studies: Cochrane says not to cite it as a reason for imprecision; GRADE has no replication rule, except through the information-size check for large effects ("Attempts to replicate results of early studies suggesting such effects often fail").
- Vote counting: Cochrane ch12 accepts direction-of-effect counting as a fallback and calls significance-based counting unacceptable; verdict's tally uses a model's reading of the result.

## Judgement calls the authors leave to a human

These are not to be mechanized; at most a labelled model answer with a reason is possible.

- Core GRADE 1 rejects mechanical rules outright: "a mechanical approach offering never-to-be-broken rules would result in many poor judgments"; after the domains an overall look is required, and a concern is a reason to rate down only if it puts the true effect on the other side of the threshold.
- Which outcome, measure and time point to take when a study offers several: the Handbook only demands a rule fixed in advance (ch5 5.3.6), it supplies none.
- Whether participants, interventions, comparators and outcomes are similar enough to pool (ch10 10.10.1); whether SD differences reflect scale rather than population (ch6 6.5.1.2); whether data are skewed; whether an SD or a median can stand in for a mean.
- Fixed or random model; whether to withhold a pooled average when estimates point in opposite directions; whether an outlying study may be excluded (almost never).
- Carry-over in crossover trials, which ICC to borrow for cluster trials, which adjusted estimate of several minimizes confounding, which non-randomized designs are strong enough, whether cohort and case-control studies ask the same question.
- The thresholds themselves: minimal important difference, "appreciable" benefit or harm, "serious" versus "very serious", how wide an interval must be for three levels (guidance 34: "a matter of value judgement").
